CHAPTER 1 · LEAD FOUNDATIONSLearn · 3 short lessons

Build the mental picture

10 electrodes. 12 electrical views.

An electrode is a physical sensor on the skin. A lead is a view of the heart’s electrical activity, derived from voltage differences.

4 limb electrodes
+
6 chest electrodes
10 sensors → 12 leadsThe recorded voltages produce six limb leads and six chest leads. “12-lead” counts views.

Memory cue: an electrode senses; a lead shows a view.

Know the two lead families

Limb leads · 6

IIIIIIaVRaVLaVF

These leads view electrical activity in the frontal plane.

Chest leads · 6

V1V2V3V4V5V6

Also called precordial leads. They view activity in the horizontal plane.

Where the electrodes go

Front view: the patient’s right is on your left. Choose an electrode below for its landmark.

Schematic positions; use the anatomical landmarks.

V1 · chest electrode

4th intercostal space, at the right border of the sternum.

An intercostal space is the gap between two ribs. V1 belongs on the patient’s right.

6 chest electrodes → 6 chest leads

V1V2V3V4V5V6

Horizontal plane. V4, V5, and V6 stay at the same horizontal level.

Placement reference

Standard diagnostic placement uses limb sites, rather than the torso sites used in some monitoring configurations. Source: Philips IntelliVue MX400–550 Instructions for Use, “Conventional 12-Lead ECG,” p. 166.

I, II, III … then aVR, aVL, aVFRemember aVL. There is no standard limb lead called IV.

One heart, two viewing planes

Frontal · limb leads

Picture a clock face in front of the heart. The six limb leads look from different directions within that vertical plane.

Horizontal · chest leads

Picture a slice across the chest. V1–V6 give complementary views within that horizontal plane.
